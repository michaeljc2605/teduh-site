Fabric guide

Know what you're wearing.

Cotton poplin, fluid twill, fine knits and statement textures. See what goes into each piece and choose what works for your day.

Four details worth checking

1. Composition
The fibre percentages tell you what each fabric contains. The Air Shirt uses cotton poplin; the Off-Shoulder Tie Top combines cotton, modal and elastane in a stretch jersey. A fibre name alone does not tell you how a finished garment will feel in the heat.
2. Construction
Compare the smooth woven surface of poplin with the texture of a fine knit or a twill. Use the product close-ups to see the weave, seams and details, and the worn photographs to judge the intended silhouette.
3. Coverage & lining
The Dot Lace Tank has a cotton-modal lining. The Pleat Sheer Dress is intentionally transparent and comes without a slip. Both faux-suede pieces have polyester linings and are best considered for aircon or evening outfits.
4. Fit & care
Check the fit notes and size guide alongside the fabric. A close-fitting jersey and a loose woven shirt sit differently on the body. Follow each garment's care instructions, particularly when drying knits or caring for pleats and brushed finishes.
Choose for your day

Start with the outfit.

Think about the time outdoors, the layers you need indoors and the pieces already in your wardrobe.

Clean basics with a statement
Pair the cotton-poplin Air Shirt with the lyocell-cotton Breeze Pant. The shirt's boxy cut and the trousers' wide leg create an easy, considered silhouette.
Texture with thrifted favourites
Try the garment-washed Rose Wash Tee with your own denim, or the mercerised-cotton Stripe Knit Polo with trousers you already wear. Use the close-ups to decide which texture suits your style.
A layer for aircon and evenings
The Suede Zip Jacket has a brushed faux-suede shell and a polyester lining. Wear it over a tee indoors, then carry it when you prefer fewer layers outside. It is not a rain jacket.

Sustainability

Wear it more. Care for it well.

Choose pieces you can combine with what you own, then follow their care instructions. Our next steps focus on sourcing, packaging and keeping clothes in use.

What we're working towards

  • Small batches. Plan limited runs to reduce unsold stock.
  • Clear sourcing. Seek documented fibre origins and certifications before making environmental claims.
  • Less packaging. Work towards paper-based mailers with less unnecessary wrapping.
  • Useful care guidance. Make garment-specific washing and storage advice easy to find.
