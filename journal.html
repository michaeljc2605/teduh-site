Campus

What to wear to uni when it's 34°C

From the walk across campus to a freezing lecture theatre, here's how to dress for both.

  1. Start with a breathable base like a quick-dry tee. Avoid thick cotton that holds sweat.
  2. Carry a light overshirt in your bag for the aircon. Lecture theatres are often set to 18–20°C.
  3. Pick mid-tone colours. They hide damp patches better than white or black.
  4. Choose shorts or wide-leg pants over skinny jeans, which trap heat.

Travel

The 5-piece Southeast Asia packing list

A carry-on wardrobe for a long weekend in Bangkok, Bali or KL.

  1. Two tops: one shirt, one tee. They mix with everything.
  2. One wide-leg pant for temples, dinners and flights.
  3. One pair of quick-dry shorts for the beach and walking.
  4. One light overshirt for planes, malls and sun cover.

Care

How to wash linen (without ruining it)

Linen gets softer with every wash, if you treat it right.

  1. Wash cold on a gentle cycle, inside out.
  2. Skip the dryer. Hang it in the shade so the colour doesn't fade.
  3. Shake it out before hanging, and most creases will fall out by themselves.
  4. Iron while slightly damp if you want a sharper look.

Work

Smart-casual for Singapore offices

Look sharp in the meeting without sweating through the walk.

  1. Swap stiff formal shirts for breathable button-ups.
  2. Keep a spare tee at your desk for very hot commute days.
  3. Wide-leg trousers look polished and keep air moving.

Lifestyle

Dressing for a hawker centre lunch

Heat from the sun, the stoves and the crowd. Here's how to stay comfortable.

  1. Loose shapes are your friend. Leave room between fabric and skin.
  2. Quick-dry fabric handles the post-laksa sweat.
  3. Darker bottoms hide the occasional sauce splash.

Weather

Surviving monsoon season in style

Sudden downpours, then steam. Dress for both.

  1. A water-resistant overshirt is the easiest upgrade.
  2. Choose fabrics that dry fast so you're not damp all afternoon.
  3. Skip pale colours that turn see-through when wet.
